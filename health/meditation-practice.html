Meditation Practice

Goals

  • Meditate 10 minutes every day
  • Perform 4-7-8 breathing before sleep each night

Why Meditation Matters for Performance

Meditation is not just stress management — it has measurable effects on athletic performance, recovery, and cognitive output:

  • Reduced cortisol: Chronic cortisol elevation impairs recovery, increases fat storage, and disrupts sleep. Mindfulness meditation reduces salivary cortisol by ~15-25% in controlled studies (Turakitwanakan et al., 2013).
  • Improved HRV: Regular meditators show higher heart rate variability, an indicator of parasympathetic tone and training readiness. Krygier et al. (2013) found a 10-day meditation program significantly increased HRV.
  • Pain and discomfort tolerance: Meditation practice increases tolerance to sustained discomfort — directly applicable to threshold and VO2max efforts on the bike. Zeidan et al. (2011) showed mindfulness practitioners reported 40% lower pain intensity ratings.
  • Attention and focus: Meta-analysis by Sedlmeier et al. (2012) found meditation reliably improves sustained attention and executive function — relevant for both deep work and pacing discipline during hard efforts.
  • Sleep quality: Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) improved sleep quality comparably to sleep hygiene education in a randomized trial (Black et al., 2015).

Daily Meditation Protocol

Recommended Practice (10 min)

Focused attention meditation is the best starting point — simpler than open monitoring and builds concentration that transfers to other tasks.

  1. Sit comfortably — chair, floor, or cushion. Spine upright but not rigid.
  2. Set a timer for 10 minutes (use a gentle chime, not an alarm).
  3. Focus on the breath — the sensation of air at the nostrils or the rise/fall of the abdomen.
  4. When the mind wanders (it will, constantly), notice the distraction without judgment and return attention to the breath. This moment of noticing and returning is the exercise — it is not a failure.
  5. End gently — when the timer sounds, open eyes slowly, take 2-3 deep breaths before standing.

Progression: After 2-4 weeks of consistent 10-min sessions, the “wandering-return” cycle lengthens naturally. You can extend to 15-20 min when 10 feels easy, but consistency matters far more than duration.

When to Meditate

  • Morning (preferred): Sets attentional tone for the day. Even 5 minutes before coffee improves subjective focus. Works well paired with a ../general/weekly-time-allocation morning routine block.
  • Pre-training: 3-5 minutes of breath focus before hard sessions can lower pre-effort anxiety and improve pacing (Thompson et al., 2011).
  • Pre-sleep: Use the 4-7-8 breathing protocol below instead of a full meditation session.

4-7-8 Breathing

Developed by Dr. Andrew Weil, based on pranayama breath control. The extended exhale activates the vagus nerve and shifts the autonomic nervous system toward parasympathetic dominance.

Protocol:

  1. Exhale completely through the mouth with a whooshing sound
  2. Close mouth, inhale through the nose for 4 seconds
  3. Hold breath for 7 seconds
  4. Exhale through the mouth for 8 seconds (whooshing sound)
  5. Repeat for 4 cycles (takes ~75 seconds total)

Why it works:

  • The extended exhale (2x the inhale) is the key mechanism — long exhalation stimulates the vagus nerve, reducing heart rate and blood pressure.
  • The breath hold increases CO2 tolerance and forces a deeper subsequent exhale.
  • The counting occupies working memory, reducing rumination that keeps you awake.

Tips:

  • Keep the tip of the tongue behind the upper front teeth throughout.
  • If holding for 7 seconds is difficult initially, maintain the 4:7:8 ratio at a shorter timescale (e.g., 2:3.5:4).
  • Practice twice daily (morning + bedtime) for the first month — Weil reports the technique becomes more effective with consistent practice over 6-8 weeks.

Other Useful Breathwork Techniques

Box Breathing (4-4-4-4)

Used by Navy SEALs for acute stress regulation:

  1. Inhale 4 seconds
  2. Hold 4 seconds
  3. Exhale 4 seconds
  4. Hold empty 4 seconds

Best for: Pre-competition anxiety, acute stress moments, calming down after a hard interval set. The equal ratios balance sympathetic and parasympathetic activation without inducing sleepiness.

Nasal-Only Breathing

Breathing exclusively through the nose (during easy/moderate exercise and daily life):

  • Increases production of nitric oxide — a vasodilator — in the nasal sinuses, improving oxygen delivery
  • Forces diaphragmatic engagement
  • May improve CO2 tolerance over time, which raises the ventilatory threshold

Research by Dallam et al. (2018) found that after 6 months of nasal-only breathing during training, runners showed improved running economy with no VO2max penalty. Applicable to Zone 1-2 cycling sessions — breathe nasally on easy rides to build tolerance.

Tracking and Accountability

  • Use a simple streak tracker — a calendar with X’s, a habit app, or a tally in the daily journal. The goal is an unbroken streak.
  • Minimum viable session: On hard days, even 2 minutes of breath focus counts. The habit of sitting down matters more than the duration.
  • If meditation is consistently skipped, the barrier is probably scheduling — anchor it to an existing habit (e.g., “after morning coffee, before opening the laptop”).
