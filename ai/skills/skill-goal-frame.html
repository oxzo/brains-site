Skill: goal-frame

Augmentation-layer orchestrator for the criteria/goal-setting operation — the second of the four higher-order operations the appreciating-position-vs-skill session decomposed, and the genuine catalog gap (existing skills audit a goal; none generated one). The AI proposes a goal structure — top-level purpose, sub-goals, weighted decision criteria, success/falsification tests — and hands it to /intent-check to audit, while the user stays the principal who ratifies the purpose and sets the weights. The regress argument is the boundary: you cannot delegate goal-setting without already holding a goal, so the AI drafts but only the principal grounds.

When to use

  • Needing to set what you’re aiming at — “help me set goals for X”, “what should the criteria be”, “what are we optimizing for”, “frame the objective”, “what does success look like”
  • A piece of work or a decision about to start with no explicit, weighted objective
  • Before generating plans or a spec, to fix the target those will serve

Skip for auditing an existing goal (/intent-check), generating plans toward an already-set goal (/plan-options), or crystallizing a converged design into a spec (/to-prd).

Why it exists

One of the four “AI performs, you stay principal” operation-skills built from the appreciating-position-vs-skill conclusion. It counters goal-by-default — inheriting an unstated, unweighted objective and then optimizing the wrong thing precisely. The PROPOSED marks and the principal gate are what keep it from being pure offloading: the AI can draft a goal tree all day, but the thing that appreciates under AI is the position that grounds it. The augmentation counterpart to /deepen-thinking’s Evaluate rung, which instead scaffolds the user to set their own criteria.
