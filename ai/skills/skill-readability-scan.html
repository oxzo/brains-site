Skill: readability-scan

Scan a prose artifact against the word- and document-level checks of the six-layer readability framework in readability-mechanics: document structure (informative headings at topic boundaries, point-first paragraphs, given-new flow, short concrete overview) and word choice (audience-familiar terms, rare-term budget with verbatim reuse, jargon the definitions can’t fix, concrete core claims), plus presentation checks only when the artifact controls its own rendering. Returns ranked findings with locations and minimum-cost fixes.

When to use

  • Before committing a substantive knowledge note, especially long or synthesis notes
  • Before publishing anything — publish-tagged notes, article drafts, HTML pages
  • When the goal is “make this nicer to read / easier to skim”
  • After a restructuring edit, to verify the new organization steers correctly

Skip when:

  • The symptom is sentence-level (monotony, missing connectives, over-compression) — that axis belongs to /density-scan
  • Journal entries, inbox captures, short notes (<~30 body lines), code
  • Deliberately voice-driven prose that delays its point on purpose

Why it exists

A note can be accurate, well-sourced, and density-clean yet still hard to use: the point buried mid-paragraph, headings that don’t predict their sections, abstract core claims, rare terms rotating through synonyms. No single sentence is wrong — the word and document levels are. The bias scans check truth, /density-scan checks sentence-level load; nothing checked whether a reader can find and absorb the content. This scan closes that gap, with every check traced to a sourced finding in the readability-mechanics note (Lorch headings line, Kieras point-first, Haviland & Clark given-new, Rayner word-cost figures, Bullock jargon-despite-definitions).
