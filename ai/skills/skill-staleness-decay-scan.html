Skill: staleness-decay-scan

Scan a note, folder, or the vault for time-sensitive notes whose perishable factual claims (version numbers, prices, model/capability state, benchmarks, “current best practice”, NCT IDs, dated statistics) have aged past their half-life and now read as authoritative while possibly being false. The skill globs notes carrying time-sensitive markers, parses their as-of/updated dates against today, assigns each perishable claim a volatility class (FAST / MEDIUM / SLOW), buckets fresh/aging/stale on half-life thresholds, and emits a ranked action table — re-verify / re-date / archive / promote-durable per stale claim. It reports the actions; it does not perform them.

When to use

  • A periodic corpus-maintenance pass — the vault has no archival outflow (git-confirmed), so staleness accumulates silently and needs a deliberate sweep
  • Before citing or building on an aging note in new work
  • After a fast-moving domain has likely shifted (a new model release, a market move, a framework major version) — scan that folder
  • Paired with /contradiction-scan on a maintenance day — this catches expiry, that catches disagreement

Skip when:

  • The scope is a queue or backlog (use /whittle-scan)
  • The item is a forecast/prediction with a resolution date — it has a per-week cost of inaction, not silently-expiring authority (use /whittle-scan or /calibration-loop)
  • The material is evergreen — a conceptual note with no dated or perishable claims is correctly out of scope (refuse-to-scan)
  • The axis is cross-note disagreement (use /contradiction-scan)

Why it exists

Silent staleness is the failure mode the scan counters — a dated claim keeps reading as current because its as-of date is invisible when you’re scanning the corpus for an answer, so the rot is never felt until a stale fact propagates into a decision. The structural reason it accumulates: the vault is append-mostly and has zero archival outflow (git-confirmed over 60 days), so the staleness stock only rises. The key design move is the volatility class — age alone is a bad signal, because a six-month-old AI-tooling claim is stale while a six-month-old physiology mechanism isn’t. Classifying FAST / MEDIUM / SLOW and bucketing against a class-specific half-life keeps the scan from flagging durable claims as noise. The load-bearing boundary is with /whittle-scan: this skill owns perishable static facts whose authority expired; /whittle-scan owns dated commitments and forecasts with a per-week cost of inaction. Forecast-resolution-dates route there, not here — the two are description-scoped apart so they don’t collide on retrieval. See knowledge-base-entropy for the backing concept.
