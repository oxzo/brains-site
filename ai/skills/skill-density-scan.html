Skill: density-scan

Scan written text for information-density failure modes that increase reader cognitive load: surprisal spikes (missing connectives), sentence-length monotony, propositional density mismatched to channel, long-distance dependencies, lexical density mismatch, and the “shorter is better” compression trap.

When to use

  • Before publishing prose where comprehension matters — knowledge notes, specs, instructions, agent briefs
  • Prompts that will run many times (small density wins compound)
  • Reviewing edits that aggressively shortened existing prose

Skip when:

  • Throwaway scratch text or chat-message prose
  • Density-tolerant readers (LLMs handle higher density than humans on some axes — see backing note)

Why it exists

Short text isn’t always better. Cuts that drop connectives, raise propositional density, or create long-distance dependencies cost the reader more than they save in length. The compression trap is the dominant failure mode: confidently-edited prose that looks tighter but reads worse. Density-scan finds where compression actually damaged comprehension, not whether to compress at all.
