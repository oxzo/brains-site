Skill: predict-then-compare

Training-layer skill: before answering a substantive query, it makes the user commit a one-line guess plus a confidence, then reveals the answer, diffs guess-vs-actual, classifies the gap, and appends the rep to journal/predict-compare-log.md. Genuine future-binary predictions are routed onward to /calibration-loop instead. The capability it builds lives in the user, not the user+AI system — it is the production-side counterpart to the verification scans, which make AI output safe to consume.

When to use

  • About to ask a substantive reasoning / estimation / judgment question and want it to double as a calibration rep
  • Wanting to commit your own answer before seeing the AI’s — “let me guess first”, “before you answer”, “quiz my judgment”
  • Building the deliberate-practice habit the backing notes prescribe — turning queries you’d make anyway into free reps

Skip when:

  • The question is a trivial lookup, syntax, or definition — no judgment to be wrong about; just answer (logging trivia floods the log)
  • The prediction is a future binary outcome with a resolution deadline — use /calibration-loop record, which owns that lifecycle
  • You only want the answer, with no intent to train (don’t invoke, or drop the rep)

Why it exists

The default AI-use loop — ask, receive, accept — trains nothing: the judgment lives in the human+AI system and evaporates when the AI is gone (the augmentation trap in augmented-vs-trained-thinking). Committing a guess first exploits the generation effect and puts skin in the game on every answer, converting consumption into a calibration rep at ~30 seconds of cost. It is that note’s candidate #1 and is flagged in skills-to-train-for-ai-future as the one trained-thinking practice to promote. The integrity rule — guess committed before the answer is revealed — is what makes the rep real; a peeked guess trains nothing.
