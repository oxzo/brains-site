Knowledge-Base Epistemic Charter and Assurance Tiers

As of 2026-09-16. The charter questions and tier structure are durable; the security-control list and the retrieval-routing table track a moving field and carry the review date above.

What it is

The practical layer under ai-knowledge-base-as-epistemic-institution: what to decide before choosing a vector database, how much assurance a given system needs, and who owns keeping it true. The engineering controls themselves — evidence plane, cache keys, answer contract, layered metrics — are in canonical-evidence-vs-derived-index and are not repeated here.

Phase 0 — start with the decision, not the technology

Before selecting a database or model, write down:

  • users, and the non-users the system’s answers will affect;
  • the decisions and actions the system will support;
  • the consequences of a false answer, an omission, a stale answer, and a refusal — separately;
  • authoritative source classes, and excluded source classes;
  • the query types expected: exact lookup, procedure, comparison, synthesis, relationship, forecast, calculation;
  • freshness, retention, deletion, and permission requirements;
  • citation granularity;
  • when the system must clarify, abstain, or escalate;
  • acceptable latency, cost, reviewer effort, and residual risk;
  • stop conditions — what result means “do not ship.”

A personal research assistant, a customer-support search tool, a legal assistant, and an operations agent need different assurance. The charter is where that difference is stated, and the two items that nobody revisits once tooling is chosen — excluded sources and stop conditions — are the reason to write it down.

The tiers are cumulative. Skipping the personal-tier baseline at enterprise scale produces an expensively governed corpus that still cannot say which note a summary came from.

Source lifecycle

Each governed source carries: stable source and version IDs; canonical URI and owner; raw snapshot and normalized form; content hash and capture time; publication, observation, valid, and record times where relevant; parser and schema versions; authority, jurisdiction, and audience; security classification and ACL metadata; license, retention, review, expiry, supersession, and deletion state.

Malformed, unsupported, unexpectedly changed, low-confidence, or suspicious sources go to quarantine — never silently into the index as partial output.

Retrieval routing by need

NeedPreferred path

Compare lexical-only, dense-only, and hybrid on the same evaluation set before choosing. Add reranking, query decomposition, contextual chunks, or agents only for a measured failure. The regime trade-offs behind the table are in knowledge-base-representation-regimes.

Security minimum

Every retrieved document, OCR result, web page, tool response, and prior model output is untrusted input. Minimum controls: an approved-source inventory with staged ingestion; identity and ACL enforcement before retrieval and ranking; deny or abstain when required policy metadata is missing or stale; data/instruction separation; parser sandboxing and active-content stripping; least-privileged tools and scoped credentials; deterministic validation of outputs and actions; human approval for consequential or irreversible actions; poisoning canaries and targeted-query tests; privacy-minimized traces; atomic rollback to a clean index generation; deletion verified across indexes, caches, exports, processors, and restored backups.

Prompts and filters are defense in depth, not a complete prompt-injection control — prompt-injection and ai-agent-security hold the argument for why.

Ownership and cadence

Minimum roles, several of which one person holds in a small system: product or service owner; domain or content owner; source steward; retrieval and ML owner; evaluation owner; operations owner; privacy, security, and legal owner; a named approver for consequential releases.

Cadence:

  • continuous — ingestion, access, deletion, freshness, latency, and error health;
  • every material change — the frozen regression and challenge suites;
  • weekly during pilot — adjudicate failures and feedback;
  • monthly — source health, drift, recurring causes, cost/latency frontier, judge calibration;
  • quarterly or after incidents — reassess risks, source authority, evaluation slices, ownership, abstention policy.

Feedback closes as a loop, not a ticket:

Three evaluation sets support that loop: a frozen regression set for comparable release gates, a rotating representative set sampled from current traffic, and a challenge set for stale, conflicting, absent, misleading, permission-blocked, injected, multilingual, multi-hop, and difficult-format cases. Automated graders are measurement instruments, not ground truth: pin their versions and prompts, test for bias, calibrate against blinded human judgment (llm-evaluation-methodology).

Adoption is part of epistemic quality

Put the system inside a real workflow, not behind an empty chat box. Show source passages and dates by default; make “report a stale or wrong source” easier than a thumbs-down; say what the system excludes and refuses; keep a non-AI fallback; and measure task completion, rework, reviewer burden, and escalation rather than message volume. A producer-side speedup that moves verification work onto another team is not an organizational gain — the accounting cognitive-burden-of-ai-use and review-capacity-org-design make.

Staged implementation

  • Phase 0 — epistemic charter. The list above. No procurement yet.
  • Phase 1 — trustworthy baseline. One bounded use case; approved sources only; canonical snapshots with version, hash, and ACL metadata; structure-aware parsing; lexical, dense, and hybrid compared; span-level citations; explicit not_found and permission-denied behavior; a carefully adjudicated set of representative and challenge cases (the essay’s 100–300 is an implementation heuristic, not a minimum); complete version, latency, and cost traces.
  • Phase 2 — close observed failures. Reranking where it improves end-to-end outcomes; parent or contextual chunks for context loss; structured tools for exact queries; conflict and valid-time handling; calibrated graders; canaries and production sampling.
  • Phase 3 — justified complexity. Only with measured need: bounded agentic retrieval; a graph or global-search path; a compiled wiki and claim registry; behavior fine-tuning; adaptive retrieval budgets; calibrated abstention; multimodal and multi-index retrieval.

Release gate

Do not call a consequential system production-ready until frozen evidence shows: reproducible source-to-answer lineage; no known critical ACL or cross-tenant failure under adversarial tests; verified deletion from canonical data and every projection; backup restoration that does not resurrect deleted content; representative retrieval and answer performance; explicit, tested conflict and abstention behavior; human-calibrated citation and grounding evaluation; acceptable latency, cost, and reviewer workload by query class; freshness and parsing-health monitoring; rollback, rebuild, and incident procedures; and named owners with human acceptance of the residual risk.

Anti-patterns

  • Treating the vector index as the source of truth.
  • Calling a retrieved or generated sentence “knowledge” without qualification.
  • Equating provenance, popularity, similarity, or citation count with truth.
  • One fixed chunk size, model, or top-k for every document and query.
  • Dense-only retrieval for identifiers, names, codes, and dates.
  • Retrieving unauthorized material and redacting after generation.
  • Adding context to compensate for poor retrieval.
  • Citations that link to a document homepage instead of a span and version.
  • Silent overwrite of a conflict or a correction.
  • Auto-ingesting generated text or raw user feedback into the canonical corpus.
  • Thumbs-up, fluency, or an uncalibrated LLM judge as correctness.
  • Recording every prompt while failing to record accepted decisions and evidence.
  • Deploying GraphRAG or agents before measuring a simpler baseline.
  • Assuming more documents mean more organizational memory.
  • Optimizing producer speed while ignoring reviewer work and downstream error.

Where this vault stands

This vault is a personal-tier system, and CLAUDE.md plus .claude/rules/ is a partial charter: it fixes source handling, the verified-or-labeled rule, and a never-fabricate abstention default, but states no excluded-source list and no stop conditions — the two items this note says nobody revisits later. Whether the rules predate the tooling cannot be checked from this clone’s history. Against the personal-tier baseline:

Baseline itemStatus

The named primary risk — entropy — is exactly what knowledge-base-entropy catalogs, and the monthly corpus-maintenance pass is the cadence row for it. All eight ownership roles collapse onto one person, which is the tier’s defining property and its defining weakness: no independent approver. The team-tier additions are what team-knowledgebase-architecture specifies for the step up.

Counterargument

Charters get written and never consulted; a roles list with eight seats is enterprise theatre for a three-person team; and the case-count heuristic is a number the source essay itself disclaims. A team that skips Phase 0 and ships a hybrid-RAG baseline with span citations has done most of the work anyway.

Mostly right on the ceremony, wrong on the two lines that matter. The excluded-source list and the stop conditions are the only parts of a charter that cannot be reconstructed from the tooling afterward, and they are the two decisions the “ship the baseline” team has made implicitly and cannot audit. For a solo system the charter collapses to a page; this vault’s is its rules directory, still missing exactly those two lines. The roles list is not eight people — it is eight questions with a name next to each, and “all of them are me” is an honest answer that names the missing approver.
