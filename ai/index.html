  • agent-output-quality-variance — The capstone over the gates and shape notes: what happens when the objective is stated as consistency of generated-code quality rather than peak quality, which is a reliability problem, not a capability one. Core reframe: gates truncate, they do not lift — a gate can only reject, so the mean rises as a consequence of cutting the left tail, the floor sits at your weakest gate’s threshold, and unaddressed defect classes keep their full original variance. Then the finding that bounds every gate-centric framework: run-to-run spread has five sources (sampling, context, specification, horizon, substrate) and gates fix exactly one — specification variance is invisible to every downstream control and plausibly dominant, while substrate variance is the one with compounding returns since gates charge rent forever and exemplar quality is bought once. Prices the stack: cost is governed by first-try pass rate, not gate runtime, and p = q^n makes it superlinear in gate count — 15 gates at q=0.90 means half of all tasks blow a 3-attempt repair budget, at which point the framework hasn’t reduced variance, it has relocated it onto the human queue.
