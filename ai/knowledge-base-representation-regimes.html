Knowledge-Base Representation Regimes

As of 2026-09-16. The regime table and the information-theoretic argument are durable; the retrieval and long-context claims cite papers from 2021–2024 whose tested models age quickly, and are marked below.

What it is

A knowledge base is not one store. It is several representations of the same underlying evidence, each strong on one axis and structurally blind on another. No single regime subsumes the rest, so a mature system routes among them rather than forcing every question through one path.

RegimeStrengthMain limitationAppropriate role
Raw documents and mediafidelity to sourcehard to query uniformlycanonical evidence
Relational / structured dataexact fields, constraints, calculationweak for ambiguous narrative meaningoperational facts and tools
Symbolic claims / knowledge graphsexplicit entities, relations, rules, pathsontology and entity-resolution costrelationships and governed claims
Lexical indexesexact terms, names, codes, datesvocabulary mismatchexact and navigational retrieval
Dense embeddingssemantic similarity and paraphraseopaque, approximate, model-dependentcandidate retrieval
Generated summaries / wikiscompression, reusable synthesisdrift, omission, flattened conflictreviewed derived knowledge
Model parametersbroad learned regularities and behaviorweak lineage, no selective deletion, stalelanguage and reasoning behavior, not canonical mutable facts

The last row is the one most often violated: facts that must be current, permissioned, or deletable do not belong in weights. fine-tuning-vs-rag holds that decision in full.

Information is not meaning, and meaning is not knowledge

Shannon information is about uncertainty reduction over a channel; semantic information is about content. A vector can preserve useful similarity while losing exact wording, negation, dates, authority, and scope — compression that improves recall and weakens auditability in the same move. The data → information → knowledge → wisdom ladder is a reminder that raw data needs interpretation, not a pipeline that runs by itself: more retrieved context does not mechanically become better judgment.

Two distinctions a governed system has to hold:

  • Mention vs use. “Source S asserted p” can be true while p is false. Misinformation may need to be stored as an object of analysis without becoming an accepted claim. The Knowledge Panel incidents in knowledge-claim-lifecycle are mention-use collapses: a vandalized assertion was ingested as a fact.
  • Summaries are transformations, not witnesses. A generated summary of a source is not independent corroboration of that source. Counting it as one is the same error as counting ten copies of a press release as ten confirmations.

Consequence: keep the source next to every compressed representation, and make each transformation reproducible.

RAG is a lossy inference pipeline

Two asymmetries follow. Evidence omitted early cannot be reconstructed by fluent generation later, however good the model. And perfect retrieval does not ensure correct synthesis. The system behaves like a noisy channel feeding an uncertain inference, so the right instrumentation is stage-specific observability plus end-to-end outcome tests — not one overall score. The layered metric table in canonical-evidence-vs-derived-index is that instrumentation.

Retrieval ranks; it does not prove

Search systems estimate relevance under incomplete judgments. Lexical and dense methods fail on different queries; rerankers buy quality with latency and cost; rank scores are not calibrated probabilities of truth. BEIR (2021) found BM25 a robust zero-shot baseline, rerankers and late-interaction models best on average at higher cost, and dense retrievers often underperforming out of domain — the empirical basis for “compare lexical, dense, and hybrid on your own evaluation set” rather than picking one on reputation (BEIR itself did not evaluate hybrids).

Operating rules:

  • treat retrieval as candidate selection for a later epistemic assessment;
  • evaluate by query type and risk slice, not aggregate;
  • measure evidence coverage and distractor exposure;
  • never convert a similarity score into a confidence.

The knowledge base is a distributed system

Even when the interface is a chat box, copies of the evidence exist in source systems, object stores, search indexes, embedding stores, graph stores, caches, prompts, traces, exports, and backups. The familiar problems follow: stale replicas, partial rebuilds, non-atomic updates, permission drift, deletion that does not propagate, restores that resurrect retired data, and version skew among parser, index, prompt, model, and policy.

The consistency split: use strong, transactional or consensus-backed updates for canonical identity, permissions, and accepted-belief transitions; let search, graph, and summary projections update asynchronously — and publish projection watermarks and source-version IDs so a reader can see staleness rather than infer it. Where “last write wins” would discard meaningful evidence, keep both versions. Immutable source versions, generation manifests, tombstones, atomic promotion, and tested deletion across every projection are the mechanisms; canonical-evidence-vs-derived-index covers them.

Evaluation is multi-objective selective prediction

The system chooses not only which answer but whether to answer, clarify, abstain, or escalate. Quality is therefore a frontier, not a point:

  • correctness and completeness;
  • evidence coverage and citation support;
  • coverage vs selective risk (how often it answers vs how often it is wrong when it does);
  • latency and cost;
  • security and privacy;
  • reviewer workload;
  • downstream task success and harm.

No scalar “RAG score” encodes those trade-offs. A release gate should be a vector of floors and ceilings, with separate floors for critical slices — see the gate in knowledge-base-epistemic-charter.

Where this vault stands

The vault occupies four of the seven regimes and deliberately skips two:

RegimeHere

Symbolic / graph[[wiki-links]] and _index.md maps — a hand-built graph with no entity resolution cost because a human resolves

Dense embeddingsnone, by design
Generated summariessnippets/ — the one generated-summary plane (the site build and index blurbs are projections too), and the one that measurably drifted (the hash fix is documented in canonical-evidence-vs-derived-index)
Model parametersClaude, which reads the notes rather than remembering them

The lossy-pipeline diagram collapses here to four stages — capture, note-writing, grep, generation — and the two that lose the most evidence are the human ones. A source read but not written up is a coverage loss; a note that paraphrases without citing is a lineage loss. Grep’s vocabulary mismatch, the lexical row’s own limitation, is the third.

Counterargument

Long-context models weaken the case for routing. If the corpus fits in the window, skip the regimes, put everything in context, and let the model do the reading — no chunker, no index, no drift. Vendors advertise windows that make most personal and many team corpora “small.”

Partly right, and the routing table already concedes it: a small, bounded, permission-homogeneous corpus is the full-context row. Three things keep the other rows alive. Permissions that differ by passage cannot be enforced inside one context. A corpus that churns invalidates the cached context on every change. And position effects — the Lost in the Middle finding that tested models used evidence unevenly by position (2023 models: GPT-3.5-Turbo, Claude-1.3, MPT-30B, LongChat-13B; published TACL 2024; must be re-tested on current ones) — mean advertised window size is not the same as reliable evidence use. The EMNLP 2024 industry comparison cuts the other way on quality: when sufficiently resourced, long-context consistently beat RAG on average, with RAG far cheaper, and its Self-Route hybrid recovered near-long-context quality at lower cost by routing on model self-reflection. So in that paper routing is a cost decision, and on quality alone it favors full context where it fits — evidence for the table’s full-context row, not for any fixed threshold.
