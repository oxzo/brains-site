Agent Fit at Task Granularity — the Delivery-Org Matrix, Re-judged

The vault’s prediction cluster argues at the level of skills and roles (skill-valuation-in-the-ai-age, human-role-roadmap-under-ai) or occupations (ai-job-displacement-timescales-mechanisms). This note works one level lower: 87 concrete delivery-org tasks, each scored for AI-agent fit, held against the vault’s framework. The task granularity is what’s new; the framework is not.

As of 2026-07-17. Fit scores are capability-relative and will drift. The re-judgments age more slowly — but not uniformly: re-judgments #1, #2, and #4 (below) are themselves indexed to the 2026 state of verification tooling, and the revise triggers at the bottom say when they fall.

Provenance and epistemic status

Source: two third-party PDF matrices (“Designer Task × Phase Matrix”, 48 tasks with per-task rationales; “Delivery Org Task × Phase Matrix”, 87 tasks — a superset adding engineering, data, security, and ops), produced outside this vault with AI assistance, against references not available to us. The artifact itself invites this treatment: “a starting hypothesis, not a verdict — filter and re-judge against your own framework.”

Consequences for how to read this note:

  • Every fit score is an unverified hypothesis. None traces to a source we can inspect. The scores are kept because the classification judgments are interesting, not because they are evidence.
  • The matrix’s own vocabulary (“re-encoding”, “origination”, “will-bound”) is close enough to this vault’s that independent convergence can’t be assumed — the same 2025–2026 consensus corpus likely sits under both. Agreement between the matrix and the vault is a consensus echo, not confirmation (skills-to-train-for-ai-future flags the identical risk).

The matrix scores each task on a single axis:

The mapping is nearly one-to-one. That’s the note’s first finding: “re-encoding vs. origination” is a compressed restatement of the vault’s valuation logic — value migrates off re-encoding because generation is cheap, and pools where someone must originate or answer for the result.

Three patterns:

  1. The phase gradient. Agent fit rises as decisions get made: Definition & Strategy and Ideation contain zero High-fit tasks between them (0 of 23), while Design & Production is majority-High (11 of 19). Fit tracks how much of the task is downstream of already-made decisions.
  2. The 11 Lows sort into two families. (a) Origination / will-bound: business model design, problem reframing, vision, scope & MVP, backlog prioritization, roadmap & portfolio shaping, voice & tone — seven tasks that are the principal seat wearing different hats. (b) Live-human / tacit: qualitative research, requirements elicitation, workshop facilitation, usability testing — four tasks whose moat is in-the-room human reading. (Sorting caveat: the designer PDF supplies rationales for 9 of the 11 and those sort cleanly; the two without — requirements elicitation and backlog prioritization — are sorted here by task nature, our judgment, not the source’s.) Two different moats, and (argued below) of different durability.
  3. The entire engineering production layer scores flat High — frontend, backend, database, data pipelines, infrastructure-as-code, CI/CD, plus test authoring and automation. Directionally consistent with the entry-level-attrition evidence in ai-job-displacement-timescales-mechanisms: the High column at task level is the “junior-shaped work” the occupation-level data shows thinning. (That source is deliberately two-sided — aggregate employment effects are ~null so far and the Jevons expansion story remains live — so this is one strand of it agreeing, not the note’s verdict.)

Where the vault re-judges

This is the “do our own analysis” part — five places the vault’s framework disagrees with or restructures the matrix.

1. The missing second axis: verification cost and blast radius

The matrix’s single biggest structural gap. human-role-roadmap-under-ai’s core claim is that delegation is per-workflow, indexed by stakes and reversibility — you are L4 on test generation and L2 on schema migrations simultaneously. A one-dimensional fit score per task type can’t express that. “Backend implementation: High” is true for the median reversible ticket and false for the auth flow or the payment path — same task type, different verification cost, different honest rung. Re-judged: fit = f(task type, verification cost, blast radius), and the matrix only models the first argument. This re-judgment is executed on the desk-relevant subset in delivery-org-matrix-desk-rescoring: no High keeps a single unconditional label, and the oracle-layer rows turn out to be delegable only under seat-governed independence.

2. “Partial” is 52% of the matrix and carries no information

45 of 87 tasks land on “agent drafts, human directs/calibrates” — which describes essentially all AI-assisted knowledge work in 2026 and therefore discriminates nothing. The vault’s ladder gives the middle its structure: which rung (L2 co-planning / L3 consult / L4 approve-by-exception), and who authors the escalation policy. Operationally, the interesting question about every Partial task is not “human in the loop?” but “what oracle does the human own, and what escalates?” (An oracle here is an independent verification check — tests, property checks, cross-model review.)

3. The High scores are near-circular — and the circularity hides origination inside production tasks

Tasks score High because they’re classified as “re-encoding existing decisions” — the score restates the classification. The classification is where the contestable judgment lives, and it bakes in an idealized decisions-then-encoding pipeline. Counter-example from the matrix itself: “Wireframing: High — re-encoding decided flows into layout” assumes the flows arrive decided; in practice wireframing is often where the deciding happens (the sketch is the argument). Real delivery work interleaves origination inside nominally-productive tasks; the waterfall framing systematically over-scores High.

4. Code review’s “Partial” collides with the rubber-stamping base rate

The matrix scores code review Partial — which, per its legend, means “agent drafts, human directs/calibrates” (no per-task rationale is given; code review appears only in the delivery-org PDF, which has none). Read through the legend, the score keeps a human judgment layer inside code review. The vault’s hardened finding says that layer is precisely what fails: automation complacency hits experts, survives practice, and is worst on highly-reliable systems (Parasuraman & Manzey 2010, via human-role-roadmap-under-ai) — rubber-stamping is the modal outcome, not a tail risk. Re-judged: code review of machine output is not a stable Partial; it’s either backed by an independent oracle the reviewer didn’t author (tests, property checks, cross-model review — making it honest) or it degrades toward an unacknowledged High with a human fig leaf.

5. Low is two claims of different durability — split it

Family (a) Lows (will-bound: vision, scope, roadmap, business model) rest on a position — someone must want the outcome and answer for it. Per appreciating-position-vs-skill, that survives capability growth by construction; these Lows are durable. Family (b) Lows (tacit/live: usability testing, qualitative research, facilitation) rest on a capability claim — “AI can’t read humans live” — and capability-based safe lists have aged badly: creativity, Go, art, and natural-sounding prose all fell off 2015-era safe lists within a decade (skill-valuation-in-the-ai-age’s counterargument section; what-survived-machine-conquest documents translation’s fall as the in-progress warning case). AI-moderated interviews and synthetic-user testing are already marketed claims (unverified here, but the direction of pressure is visible). Re-judged: family (a) Low is a floor; family (b) Low is a forecast — track them separately.

Two qualifications on the floor, so the positional argument isn’t quietly converted into a task-count argument it can’t support. First: the floor guarantees the function survives — someone originates and answers — not that seven tasks’ worth of principal-work persists, or any given seat’s. Will-bound work can consolidate into fewer chairs. Second: even the position carries appreciating-position-vs-skill’s own live erosion trigger, AI systems bearing real institutional accountability. The same split applies inside the Low rationale “origination of brand identity” (voice & tone): the identity decision is family (a); the generation of candidate voices is already Partial.

The split’s most uncomfortable product comes from pointing it at the Partial column. “Agent drafts, human directs/calibrates” is itself a capability claim — a statement about what agents can’t yet do unassisted — so every Partial whose human half is not will-bound is family (b) by construction. On this matrix’s own terms, the only floor is the seven will-bound Lows. The 45-task Partial middle is a forecast wearing a reassuring label.

Where the matrix pushes back on the vault

Re-judging in one direction only would be its own bias — the task granularity does expose two things the vault’s skill/role-level cluster can’t do. Both inherit the provenance discount: they come from the same unverifiable source the re-judgments discount when it agrees with the vault. So they are framed as questions the vault should answer, not findings the matrix established:

  1. The vault has no task inventory. Every cluster note argues about which skills appreciate, but none can answer “which of the tasks on my desk this week are delegable, and at what rung?” The matrix’s contribution is the census structure, honestly labeled: a genre-typical delivery-org enumeration — consultancy-shaped task names, waterfall phases — not anyone’s verified inventory. As a scaffold to inventory real work against, it is still the first of its kind here. human-role-roadmap-under-ai says rungs are per-workflow; this is the first artifact that enumerates candidate workflows to point that question at.
  2. A denominator question no vault note poses. Taken at face value, 11 of 87 tasks (~13%) are origination-bound — a small share against the vault’s reassuring prose about value migrating to judgment and accountability. The number is demoted on arrival: task counts are not time or value weights, and the count is unstable under re-slicing. It is a property of this enumeration, not a measurement. What survives the demotion is the question. The vault argues the migration’s direction without ever stating a denominator, and under any plausible slicing the origination-bound share of a delivery org’s task list is a minority. One rationale from the designer PDF is worth keeping verbatim as this point’s sharpest instance: documentation & artifact maintenance as “the durable, illegible job — agents keep it current.”

The read for an architecture-owning engineering seat

What the matrix implies for the seat that owns solution architecture, tech-stack selection, data modeling, and estimation — stated with re-judgment #5 applied consistently rather than comfortably:

  • Every task nearest that seat sits Partial, and every one of those Partials is capability-bound — family (b). None is will-bound; the will-bound Lows (scope, backlog, roadmap, vision) read as sitting one seat up, with product/PO roles. (Role attribution is our inference from task nature — the PDFs advertise role tags but the rendered tables don’t show per-row roles.) The uncomfortable conclusion the split forces: the architecture seat’s moat is a forecast, not a floor. skill-valuation-in-the-ai-age already carries the sentence that applies here, and it is easy to leave behind in that note: senior judgment is “an appreciating asset built on a depreciating ladder… better verification tooling erodes the moat from the other side.”
  • The strategic implication is therefore a choice, not reassurance. One option is to move toward the will-bound seat: own outcomes — scope, priority, the answer-for-it role — not only the technical decisions. The other is to make the Partials survivable by owning the oracle: per human-role-roadmap-under-ai, the seat that authors the verification architecture keeps a durable function even as its tasks drift from Partial toward High-with-oracle. “Keep doing architecture and stay Partial” is not one of the stable options this matrix’s own logic leaves open.
  • The actionable cut from re-judgment #2: for each Partial task actually on the desk, the move is not “use AI more” but “decide the rung and build the oracle” — the roadmap note’s per-workflow question restated at task level.
  • Re-judgment #3 is the working caution in the other direction: tasks where origination hides inside production (architecture decisions made while “just implementing” a spike) are exactly where a flat High/Partial label would misprice the seat’s work — some of the moat is real but mislabeled by the taxonomy.

What would revise this note

  • A verified source list from the matrix’s author materially different from the 2025–2026 consensus corpus → the consensus-echo caveat weakens; the matrix becomes independent evidence rather than a restatement.
  • Family (b) Lows falling in practice (AI-moderated usability/qual research producing decisions teams actually ship on) → the Low family split is confirmed in the uncomfortable direction and the remaining Lows compress to the will-bound seven.
  • Verification tooling maturing to where the engineering-High column routinely ships unreviewed (skill-valuation-in-the-ai-age’s second falsifier) → re-judgment #1’s second axis collapses; fit really does become one-dimensional, and the matrix was right ahead of schedule.
  • Field evidence that the phase gradient inverts — agents doing well-bounded strategy drafting while production stalls on integration cost → the re-encoding definition of High was wrong, not just circular.
  • Will-bound work visibly consolidating into fewer seats (one principal plus agents holding vision + scope + backlog + roadmap across what were several roles) → family (a) held as a function but failed as a headcount floor; strip any remaining reassurance-reading from the floor language.
