Prose Revision Operations — What Sentence Surgery Is Evidence-Based

readability-mechanics explains why text is hard to read; information-density-in-written-text covers the sentence-level density axis. This note covers a third question those two don’t answer: which revision operations — edits applied to existing text without changing its meaning — have evidence of improving comprehension. It is the evidence base for the /prose-surgery skill, the fix-mode counterpart to the two diagnostic scans.

The distinction matters because most revision advice is craft lore (Strunk-and-White-style injunctions with no outcome data), and some operations that feel like improvements cut both ways. The catalog below sorts operations into three tiers by evidence strength.

Tier 1 — direct revision evidence (text revised, comprehension measured)

Repair inference calls: explicit referents, argument overlap, repeated linking words

The strongest result in this literature. Britton & Gülgöz (1991) took an instructional text, identified the points where Kintsch’s comprehension model predicted readers must generate a bridging inference (an “inference call”), and repaired each one — making implicit referents explicit, increasing argument overlap between adjacent sentences, and repeating linking words instead of rotating synonyms. Two experiments (170 undergraduates; 125 Air Force recruits) found the principled revision improved both free recall and deep-comprehension measures over the original. This is direct evidence for three operations at once:

  • Name the referent instead of relying on “this/that/it” across a sentence boundary.
  • Reuse the same term for the same concept (argument overlap) — the revision-side twin of the scans’ synonym-rotation checks.
  • Open sentences on given information, put new information late (the given-new contract, covered mechanistically in readability-mechanics).

Population caveat: instructional text read by students/recruits for later recall — reference and persuasive genres are extrapolation.

Tier 2 — mechanism evidence (processing cost measured, revision effect inferred)

Shorten open dependencies; split sentences that hold clauses open

Gibson’s Dependency Locality Theory (Gibson 1998, Cognition; Gibson 2000): integration cost rises with the distance between dependent elements (e.g., subject and verb), and incomplete dependencies impose a storage cost while held open. Locality effects consistent with this appear across English, German, Chinese, Russian, and Hindi. The revision operations this licenses: close the subject-verb gap (move interrupting material out), and split a sentence whose reader must hold several incomplete clauses at once.

Counter-evidence, load-bearing: anti-locality effects are also documented — in several constructions, more pre-verb material speeds processing at the verb, explained by expectation/surprisal (readers predict the verb better). So “shorter dependencies” is not a universal rule; the honest trigger is a dependency the reader cannot predict across the gap, not distance per se.

Move parentheticals out of the sentence’s spine

Two converging strands, neither a direct within-sentence revision experiment (flagged as a bridge): DLT’s storage cost applies to material held open across an interruption; and the text-interruption literature (“Interruptions Disrupt Reading Comprehension,” JEP: General; Ledoux & Gordon 2002) finds interruptions degrade comprehension specifically when the task requires integrating information across the text, with working-memory maintenance the proposed mechanism. The operation: an aside that interrupts a claim moves to the end of the sentence or into its own sentence. The bridge from between-text interruptions to within-sentence parentheticals is analogical — treat the operation as mechanism-plausible, not proven.

Reverse nominalizations where the actor matters

Wolfer (2016) studied nominalization structures as one of legal language’s documented difficulty sources, using the Freiburg Legalese Reading Corpus (eye-tracking on natural legal German, with reformulated variants built for comparison); nominalization removes the actor from the sentence, and comprehension-oriented reformulation restores verb + actor. Direction of evidence supports the operation “unpack the nominalization into who-does-what” when the actor is informative; the specific effect sizes were not re-verified for this note. Counter-case: a nominalization that compresses an already-established action into given information (“this decision”) serves the given-new contract — reversing those is harmful, which is why the trigger is “actor obscured,” not “nominalization present.”

Tier 3 — writer-training evidence (transfers to revision by assumption)

Sentence combining — the inverse operation exists and has the better trial record

Graham & Perin’s Writing Next meta-analysis (2007; 123 documents, 154 effect sizes, grades 4–12): sentence-combining instruction improves writing quality at ES 0.50, while traditional grammar instruction lands at −0.32. Two honest limits: this measures writer training, not revision of a given text; and the population is adolescent students. But it earns its place for the asymmetry it proves: choppy, relation-hiding sequences of short sentences are a real defect, and the fix is combining — the exact inverse of Tier 2’s splitting. Any revision skill that only ever splits sentences is running half the catalog and will over-fragment.

What this implies for a revision skill

  1. Operations need triggers, not preferences. Every operation above has a counter-case (anti-locality, given-new nominalizations, over-fragmentation). The unit of the skill is symptom → operation, never “apply operation everywhere.”
  2. Meaning preservation is the hard constraint — Britton & Gülgöz worked precisely because the revision changed inference load, not content. Facts, hedges, citations, and links survive verbatim; only sentence mechanics move.
  3. The evidence supports a small catalog done carefully over a long checklist: referent repair, term reuse, given-new ordering (Tier 1); dependency shortening, parenthetical extraction, actor restoration (Tier 2); combining as the counter-move (Tier 3).
  4. Voice is out of scope. Nothing above licenses register changes, vocabulary substitution beyond referent/term repair, or cutting content — those alter meaning or authorship, and no comprehension result requires them.
